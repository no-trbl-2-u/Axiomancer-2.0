# Game Mechanics Recommendations for Axiomancer

## Overview
Axiomancer is a philosophy and ethics-based TTRPG with three core stats (Heart, Body, Mind) and a bright-to-dark emotional progression. This document recommends new mechanics inspired by other TTRPGs and philosophical concepts, all themed around logical fallacies.

---

## Design Principles

### Core Themes
- **Philosophy & Ethics**: Every mechanic should reflect philosophical concepts
- **Fallacy-Based**: Skills, equipment, and systems based on logical fallacies
- **Emotional Arc**: Bright/hopeful early game → dark/devastating late game
- **Heart/Body/Mind**: All mechanics tie to these three stats

### Inspiration from Other TTRPGs
- **D&D 5e**: Advantage/disadvantage system, spell slots
- **Darkest Dungeon**: Stress/affliction system, light/darkness
- **Disco Elysium**: Thought cabinet, skill checks as internal dialogue
- **Mörk Borg**: Doom mechanics, bleak aesthetics
- **Persona series**: Social links affecting power
- **Undertale**: Mercy mechanics, consequences for violence

## 1. The Authenticity Metric System

### Concept
A core stat that represents your character's philosophical integrity and connection to truth. It decays as you use fallacies and grows through virtuous reasoning. Inspired by Mörk Borg's doom clock and Darkest Dungeon's stress.

### Mechanics

**Authenticity Score**: 0-100 scale
- **100-80 (Enlightened)**: 
  - +2 to all virtue skill rolls
  - Can see through enemy fallacies
  - Bright visual effects
  - NPCs trust you
  
- **79-50 (Compromised)**:
  - Normal gameplay
  - Some self-doubt status effects
  
- **49-20 (Corrupted)**:
  - -1 to virtue skills, +1 to fallacy skills
  - Dark visual filters
  - NPCs suspicious
  - Occasional intrusive thoughts (random debuffs)
  
- **19-0 (Hollow)**:
  - Cannot use virtue skills
  - Fallacy skills gain +3 damage but cost HP
  - World appears gray
  - NPCs flee or attack on sight
  - Risk of "Bad Faith Breakdown" (see below)

### Authenticity Changes
- **Using Fallacy Skills**: -1 to -5 per use (scaling with skill power)
- **Using Virtue Skills**: +1 to +3 per use
- **Winning via Compassion**: +5
- **Killing enemies**: -3
- **Making friends via Defense+Defense**: +10
- **Lying in dialogue**: -2
- **Philosophical honesty in dialogue**: +2
- **Meditation/Rest at shrines**: +15

### Fallacy Integration
- **Fallacy**: Self-deception, bad faith (Sartre), cognitive dissonance
- **Equipment**: 
  - *Mirror of Authenticity* - Shows current score, +5 willpower vs. corruption
  - *Mask of False Virtue* - Hides low authenticity from NPCs but decreases it faster
  - *The Philosopher's Stone* - Rare item that prevents authenticity loss for 10 turns

### Late Game Twist
At Authenticity 0, you face a choice:
1. **Embrace the Void** - Become a devastating fallacy-wielding force, lose access to virtue skills permanently
2. **Seek Redemption** - Quest to restore authenticity through painful self-confrontation

## 2. Cognitive Dissonance System

### Concept
Holding contradictory beliefs causes mental strain. Inspired by D&D's exhaustion levels and Darkest Dungeon's stress.

### Mechanics

**Contradictions Tracked**: The game remembers your choices and beliefs expressed in dialogue.

**Dissonance Level**: 0-5
- **Level 0**: Clear conscience
- **Level 1**: -1 to Mind checks, occasional hesitation in dialogue
- **Level 2**: -2 to Mind checks, intrusive thoughts during combat (random text pop-ups)
- **Level 3**: -2 to all stats, nightmares when resting (lose HP instead of gaining)
- **Level 4**: Random "Frozen" turns (can't act due to internal conflict)
- **Level 5**: **Cognitive Collapse** - Forced to confront your contradictions in a special psychological boss fight

### Gaining Dissonance
- Saying "killing is wrong" then killing someone: +2
- Using fallacy X to attack, virtue Y to defend (if incompatible): +1
- Equipping items that contradict your stated philosophy: +1
- NPCs calling out your hypocrisy: +1

### Reducing Dissonance
- Honest self-reflection dialogues: -1
- Aligning actions with stated beliefs: -1
- Meditation: -2
- Confessing contradictions to NPCs: -3 (but social consequences)

### Fallacy Integration
- **Fallacy**: Cognitive dissonance (Festinger), tu quoque (hypocrisy)
- **Skills**:
  - *Rationalization* (Mind) - Spend 20 MP to reduce dissonance by 1
  - *Doublethink* (Mind) - Hold contradictory beliefs without dissonance for 5 turns, but +3 dissonance after
  - *Self-Awareness* (Virtue) - Reveals your contradictions, heavy dissonance reduction but painful

## 3. Socratic Dialogue System

### Concept
NPCs aren't just quest-givers; they're philosophical opponents. Dialogue is a strategic skill-check minigame. Inspired by Disco Elysium's skill checks and Persona's social links.

### Mechanics

**Dialogue Stance System**: Choose your approach
- **Socratic Questioning** (Mind) - Ask probing questions to reveal flaws
- **Empathetic Listening** (Heart) - Build trust through understanding
- **Empirical Evidence** (Body) - Use facts and observations
- **Rhetorical Persuasion** (Heart) - Emotional appeals
- **Logical Deconstruction** (Mind) - Break down their argument piece by piece

**Opposed Skill Checks**: 
- Your stat + 1d20 vs. NPC's stat + 1d20
- Success: Gain insight, trust, or information
- Failure: NPC becomes defensive, closes off, or attacks verbally

**Fallacy Recognition**: During dialogue, you can spend Mind points to identify when an NPC uses a fallacy. Call it out for:
- Social advantage (they respect your insight)
- Tactical advantage (if dialogue leads to combat, start with advantage)
- Truth revealed (they admit what they're hiding)

**Relationship Depth System**:
- NPCs remember every conversation
- Repeated dialogues unlock deeper philosophical discussions
- High relationship → NPCs teach you skills, give rare items, or join your quest
- Low relationship → NPCs spread rumors, refuse help, or become enemies

### Fallacy Integration
- **Fallacies**: Socratic method, dialectic, appeal to emotion, appeal to authority
- **Rewards**:
  - *Philosophical Bond* (Heart skill) - Learned from befriending NPCs at high authenticity
  - *Insight* (Mind buff) - Gained from successfully deconstructing arguments
  - *Social Capital* - Currency earned through virtuous dialogues, spent on special items

## 4. Virtue vs. Fallacy Skill Trees

### Concept
Two parallel progression paths that reflect your moral choices. Inspired by Infamous's karma system and Fable's morality.

### Mechanics

**Dual Skill Trees**:
- **Virtue Tree**: Compassion, Logic, Honesty, Courage, Wisdom
  - Bright, healing, defensive, buff-focused
  - Requires high Authenticity to unlock higher tiers
  - Example: *Philosopher's Patience* - Cooldown-based, guaranteed critical hit after 3 defensive turns
  
- **Fallacy Tree**: Deception, Manipulation, Cruelty, Cowardice, Ignorance
  - Dark, damaging, offensive, debuff-focused
  - Requires low Authenticity to unlock higher tiers
  - Example: *Gaslighting Mastery* - Make enemy question reality, causes Confusion + Paranoia

**Cross-Contamination**: Using opposite tree skills causes dissonance
- Virtue user suddenly using fallacy: +2 dissonance
- Fallacy user attempting virtue: Skill fails, -10 MP wasted

**Ultimate Skills** (Level 20+):
- **Virtue Ultimate**: *Dialectical Unity* - Merge all three aspects (Heart/Body/Mind) into perfect synthesis, massive heal + buff + cleanse all debuffs
- **Fallacy Ultimate**: *Existential Nihilism* - Declare all meaning void, massive AoE damage that also hurts you and lowers Authenticity to 0

### Fallacy Integration
- Every skill in the tree IS a fallacy or virtue
- Equipment can only be used if you meet Virtue/Fallacy alignment requirements
- Late game choice: Redeem yourself (switch trees) or embrace corruption (lock into fallacy tree)

## 5. The Burden of Knowledge System

### Concept
The more you learn about the world's true nature, the heavier the psychological weight. Inspired by Lovecraftian horror and Bloodborne's Insight mechanic.

### Mechanics

**Knowledge Stat**: Hidden counter that tracks dark truths learned
- Discovering the pantheon's true nature: +10
- Learning why the labyrinth exists: +15
- Understanding your role in the world's fate: +20
- Reading forbidden texts: +5 per text
- Witnessing existential horrors: +variable

**Burden Effects** (Knowledge Threshold):
- **0-20**: Blissful ignorance, normal gameplay
- **21-40**: *The Weight Begins*
  - Occasional dark visions during rest
  - +1 Mind Defense (understanding) but -1 Heart Defense (despair)
- **41-60**: *Existential Dread*
  - Visual distortions (screen darkens)
  - Random "heavy sigh" animations
  - NPCs comment on your "haunted look"
  - Sleep requires longer rest periods
- **61-80**: *Cosmic Despair*
  - Combat advantage against "ignorant" enemies (they don't know what you know)
  - Disadvantage against enlightened enemies (they see your suffering)
  - Can't use Heart-based virtue skills (too heavy to feel compassion)
- **81-100**: *The Abyss Gazes Back*
  - Unlock *Forbidden Knowledge* skill tree
  - Permanent -5 to all Heart stats
  - Can perceive hidden truths in world (secret areas, true enemy forms)
  - NPCs afraid of you
  - Special ending path unlocked

### Mechanical Trade-Off
- **Power vs. Sanity**: Knowledge gives strategic advantages but makes the world darker
- Can share knowledge with NPCs to reduce your burden (-5 Knowledge) but it burdens them

### Fallacy Integration
- **Fallacy**: Appeal to ignorance, burden of proof, argument from incredulity
- **Skills Unlocked by High Knowledge**:
  - *Unveil Truth* - Force enemy to see what you see, massive Mind damage
  - *Willful Ignorance* (Mind) - Temporarily reduce Knowledge by 20 for mental peace
  - *The Unspoken Horror* - Whisper forbidden knowledge, enemy becomes Terrified or goes insane

## 6. Philosophical Stances System

### Concept
Your declared philosophical worldview affects gameplay. Inspired by D&D alignment and Tyranny's reputation system.

### Mechanics

**Four Stances** (Choose one during character creation, can change through play):

1. **Rationalism** (Mind-focused)
   - +2 Mind, -1 Heart
   - Skills: Deductive reasoning, logical immunity, a priori insights
   - Weakness: Vulnerable to emotional appeals
   - Equipment: Require high Mind stat
   - Playstyle: Strategic, defensive, puzzle-solving

2. **Empiricism** (Body-focused)
   - +2 Body, -1 Mind
   - Skills: Sensory perception, physical evidence attacks, skepticism shield
   - Weakness: Vulnerable to abstract concepts
   - Equipment: Require physical proof to equip
   - Playstyle: Aggressive, explorative, evidence-gathering

3. **Idealism** (Heart-focused)
   - +2 Heart, -1 Body
   - Skills: Will manifestation, consciousness attacks, transcendence
   - Weakness: Vulnerable to material reality
   - Equipment: Must align with ideals
   - Playstyle: Support, diplomacy, conviction-based

4. **Materialism** (Body-focused, different from Empiricism)
   - +2 Body, -1 Mind
   - Skills: Physical dominance, denial of abstract, reductionism
   - Weakness: Vulnerable to non-physical attacks
   - Equipment: Only tangible items
   - Playstyle: Tank, brute force, resource-gathering

**Stance Drift**: Your actions can shift your stance
- Use Mind skills repeatedly → Drift toward Rationalism
- Use Heart skills repeatedly → Drift toward Idealism
- Changing stances mid-game causes temporary stat penalties (identity crisis)

**NPC Reactions**: 
- Rationalist NPCs respect Rationalist players
- Opposing stances create tension but interesting dialogue
- Some quests only available to certain stances

### Fallacy Integration
- Each stance has unique fallacies they're prone to
- **Rationalist fallacy**: Overreliance on logic (cold, heartless choices)
- **Empiricist fallacy**: Dismissing non-observable truths
- **Idealist fallacy**: Ignoring physical suffering for ideals
- **Materialist fallacy**: Reducing consciousness to matter

## 7. Emotional Resonance Combat

### Concept
Combat isn't just physical; it's emotional. Attacks carry emotional weight. Inspired by Persona's weakness system and Undertale's ACT system.

### Mechanics

**Emotional States** (tracked per combatant):
- **Calm**: Neutral state
- **Inspired**: +2 to virtue skills, +10% healing
- **Enraged**: +2 to fallacy skills, -1 defense
- **Despairing**: -2 to all skills, can't flee
- **Hopeful**: Immunity to fear effects, +1 Heart Defense
- **Joyful**: +1 to all stats, infectious (spreads to allies)

**Emotional Damage**: 
Skills can target emotions instead of (or in addition to) HP:
- *Ad Hominem* - Deals HP damage + inflicts Despairing
- *Compassionate Listening* - Heals + inflicts Calm (good for de-escalation)
- *Appeal to Hope* - No HP damage but inflicts Hopeful

**Emotional Combos**:
- If enemy is Despairing, Heart attacks deal double damage
- If enemy is Enraged, Mind attacks have advantage
- If enemy is Calm, Body attacks are resisted

**Emotional Overflow**:
- Extreme emotions cause special effects:
  - **Peak Despair** → Enemy surrenders or begs for mercy
  - **Peak Joy** → Combat ends in friendship
  - **Peak Rage** → Enemy becomes berserk (powerful but reckless)

### Fallacy Integration
- Each fallacy causes specific emotional states
- Equipment can grant emotional immunity or amplify emotional effects
- Late game enemies can be emotionally immune (nihilistic, hollow)

## 8. Memory & Consequence System

### Concept
The world remembers everything you do. Inspired by Undertale's genocide route and Tyranny's conquest system.

### Mechanics

**Karma Tracker** (hidden from player initially):
- Every action has moral weight
- Killing: +1 to +5 Karma (depending on enemy type)
- Sparing/Befriending: -1 to -5 Karma
- Using cruel fallacies: +1 Karma
- Using virtues: -1 Karma

**World Reactions by Karma Level**:
- **0-20 Karma (Saint)**: 
  - NPCs offer help freely
  - Prices lower
  - Special "Saint Ending" available
  - Unlocks *Aura of Virtue* passive
  
- **21-50 Karma (Neutral)**:
  - Normal gameplay
  
- **51-100 Karma (Sinner)**:
  - NPCs hostile
  - Prices higher or refuse service
  - Guards attack on sight
  - Unlocks *Aura of Malice* passive
  
- **101+ Karma (Irredeemable)**:
  - Entire world turns against you
  - Special enemies hunt you
  - Can only interact with other corrupted beings
  - *Point of No Return* - Can't redeem without extreme measures
  - Unlocks "Dark Philosopher" ending

**Memory-Based Encounters**:
- Kill an NPC's friend → They remember and ambush you later
- Spare an enemy → They may help you in the future
- Use a fallacy against someone → They bring it up later ("You said X, but now you do Y!")

### Fallacy Integration
- **Fallacy**: Consequentialism vs. deontology (ends vs. means)
- Late game moral dilemma: Achieve "good" outcome through fallacies/cruelty, or fail virtuously?

## 9. Corruption of Innocence Mechanic

### Concept
Reflects the bright-to-dark arc. Your character starts optimistic but the world breaks them. Inspired by Darkest Dungeon's hamlet upgrades and Spec Ops: The Line's descent.

### Mechanics

**Innocence Score**: 100 at start, decreases with story progression
- **100-80 (Child-like Wonder)**:
  - Bright colors, cheerful music
  - NPCs protective of you
  - Combat avoidable through dialogue
  - World seems magical
  
- **79-50 (Growing Awareness)**:
  - Colors desaturate slightly
  - Music becomes melancholic
  - Some NPCs manipulate your innocence
  - First required combat encounter
  
- **49-20 (Disillusionment)**:
  - Dark, muted colors
  - Music turns ominous
  - NPCs no longer hide harsh truths
  - Combat unavoidable
  - Dialogue options become cynical
  
- **19-0 (Broken)**:
  - Black and white world
  - Droning, oppressive soundtrack
  - NPCs treat you as "one of us now"
  - Combat is brutal, no mercy options
  - Dialogue is nihilistic or rage-filled
  - Can access "Point of No Return" area

**Innocence Loss Triggers** (Automatic, story-driven):
- Witnessing betrayal: -10
- First kill: -15
- Learning dark truth about pantheon: -20
- Labyrinth revelations: -30
- Final revelation (endgame): -25

**Cannot Be Recovered**: Unlike Authenticity, Innocence is permanent loss
- This is the "bright to dark" arc
- Represents loss of childhood, naivety, hope
- Endgame choice: Accept darkness or rage against it

### Fallacy Integration
- **Fallacy**: Loss of innocence, loss of faith, nihilism, despair
- **Equipment unlocked at low innocence**:
  - *The Broken Compass* - Equipment from Equip-Compendium
  - *Cynic's Monocle* - See world's "true" ugly form, +5 perception -5 Heart
  - *Shattered Ideals* - Weapon that scales with innocence loss

## 10. Social Proof & Groupthink System

### Concept
Fallacies about following the crowd vs. standing alone. Inspired by mass effect's paragon/renegade and Dragon Age's approval.

### Mechanics

**Influence vs. Independence**:
Two opposing stats:
- **Influence**: How much you sway others, social power
- **Independence**: How resistant to peer pressure, self-reliant

**Trade-Off**:
- High Influence, Low Independence: Popular but conformist
  - NPCs agree with you easily
  - Can recruit companions
  - Vulnerable to groupthink debuffs
  
- Low Influence, High Independence: Isolated but authentic
  - NPCs respect but don't follow
  - Solo bonuses
  - Immune to social pressure

**Group Combat**:
- With companions: Share buffs, can combo attacks
- BUT: If one falls to fallacy (fear, rage, etc.), others affected
- Groupthink debuff: All make same tactical mistakes

**Standing Alone**:
- Solo combat: +2 to all stats, unique skills
- No backup, higher risk
- Special "Lone Philosopher" skills

### Fallacy Integration
- **Fallacies**: Bandwagon, appeal to popularity, appeal to tradition
- **Skills**:
  - *Bandwagon Technique* - Convince enemy their side is losing, inflict Cowardice
  - *Iconoclast's Resolve* - Stand against crowd, +5 to all stats when outnumbered
  - *Herd Mentality* - Force enemies to copy each other's actions (1 turn)

## 11. The Philosopher's Inventory

### Concept
Items aren't just stat sticks; they represent philosophical concepts. Inspired by Dark Souls item descriptions and Planescape: Torment's item lore.

### Mechanics

**Philosophical Equipment Types**:

1. **Epistemic Items** (Knowledge-based)
   - *The Skeptic's Lens* - See through illusions, +5 Mind, -3 Heart (doubt everything, including hope)
   - *Occam's Razor* - Weapon, damage scales with simplest solution to puzzle
   - *The Cartesian Compass* - Points toward certainty, grants navigation bonus

2. **Ethical Items** (Morality-based)
   - *Kant's Categorical Imperative* - Shield, blocks any action you wouldn't universalize
   - *The Trolley Problem* - Consumable, forces you to choose who dies
   - *Utilitarian Scales* - Weighs outcomes, shows optimal "greatest good" choice

3. **Existential Items** (Being-based)
   - *Sisyphus's Boulder* - Heavy weapon, high damage, represents absurd struggle
   - *The Void's Whisper* - Accessory, hear nihilistic truths, +Mind -Authenticity
   - *Kierkegaard's Leap* - Boots, enables "leap of faith" (teleport to unseen area)

4. **Fallacy-Forged Items** (From Equip-Compendium)
   - Already documented: Vile Flute, Wretch's Mirror, etc.
   - Each has philosopher attribution

**Item Synergies**:
- Equipping items with contradictory philosophies: +Dissonance
- Equipping items from same philosophical school: Set bonus
- Example: Equipping all Empiricist items → "Sense-Data Mastery" (perceive all hidden objects)

### Fallacy Integration
- Items ARE fallacies/concepts
- Reading item descriptions increases Knowledge stat
- Can destroy items for philosophical insights (learn skill from item)

## 12. Fate vs. Free Will Mechanic

### Concept
Meta-commentary on player choice in games. Inspired by Bioshock's "would you kindly" and Stanley Parable.

### Mechanics

**Destiny Points**: Hidden mechanic
- Game tracks moments where you follow the "expected" path vs. deviate
- Following quest markers, main story: +Destiny
- Exploring off-path, refusing quests: +Free Will

**Narrative Acknowledgment**:
- At high Destiny: NPCs comment "You're doing exactly what you're supposed to"
- At high Free Will: NPCs confused, "That's not what I expected"
- Game "pushes back" at high Free Will (harder enemies, NPCs hostile)

**Late Game Revelation**:
- You discover the labyrinth is a test of free will
- Your Destiny vs. Free Will score determines ending
- High Destiny → "Dutiful Ending" (become King's advisor, lose self)
- High Free Will → "Rebel Ending" (reject system, uncertain but authentic)
- Balanced → "Synthesis Ending" (create third option)

**Mechanical Impact**:
- High Destiny: Quests easier, clear objectives, guided experience
- High Free Will: Quests harder, obscure objectives, emergent gameplay

### Fallacy Integration
- **Fallacy**: Determinism vs. free will, false dichotomy (are these the only options?)
- **Skills**:
  - *Predestination* - See "fated" outcome, can choose to follow or defy
  - *Authentic Choice* - Break free from expected behavior, reset combat encounter
  - *The Absurd Rebellion* - Ultimate skill, declare free will in determined world

## 13. Language & Rhetoric System

### Concept
Words have power. How you phrase arguments matters. Inspired by Scribblenauts and Disco Elysium's skill system.

### Mechanics

**Rhetorical Modes** (equippable in dialogue):
1. **Ethos** (Credibility) - Uses Heart, establishes authority
2. **Pathos** (Emotion) - Uses Heart, appeals to feelings
3. **Logos** (Logic) - Uses Mind, uses reason
4. **Kairos** (Timing) - Uses Body (reflexes), seizes opportune moment

**Argument Construction**:
- Dialogue choices aren't just "option A/B/C"
- You construct arguments: [Mode] + [Evidence] + [Conclusion]
- Example: [Ethos] + [Show credentials] + [Trust my expertise] = High persuasion
- Example: [Logos] + [Cite fallacy] + [Therefore you're wrong] = Deconstruct opponent

**Rhetorical Fallacies Can Be Used Intentionally**:
- Select "Use Fallacy" option
- Choose from learned fallacies
- NPC might not notice (persuasion success)
- NPC might call you out (backfire, lose credibility)
- Decreases Authenticity

**Language Skills**:
- *Eloquence* (Heart) - Passive bonus to all dialogue
- *Technical Jargon* (Mind) - Confuse opponents with complex terms
- *Plain Speech* (Body) - Cut through pretense, honesty bonus
- *Sophistry* (Fallacy) - Master of misleading rhetoric

### Fallacy Integration
- Every rhetorical fallacy in the game can be used in dialogue
- NPCs use fallacies too, you can call them out
- Becoming a master rhetorician is a viable build path

## 14. The Labyrinth as Metaphor System

### Concept
The labyrinth isn't just a dungeon; it's psychological. Inspired by Silent Hill's manifestation and Persona's cognitive palaces.

### Mechanics

**Labyrinth Reflects Your Mind**:
- High Authenticity → Labyrinth has clear paths, helpful signs
- Low Authenticity → Labyrinth twists, doors lead nowhere, whispers in walls
- High Knowledge → Labyrinth reveals its true purpose (cosmic horror)
- High Innocence → Labyrinth seems like an adventure
- Low Innocence → Labyrinth is oppressive, claustrophobic

**Personalized Encounters**:
- Enemies are manifestations of your psychological state
- High Dissonance → Fight "Your Contradictions" boss (multi-headed hydra)
- Low Authenticity → Fight "The Liar You've Become" shadow self
- Guilt from killing → Ghosts of those you killed haunt you

**Rooms as Philosophical Concepts**:
- "The Room of Infinite Regress" - Mirrors reflecting mirrors, Mind puzzle
- "The Chamber of the Trolley Problem" - Forced moral choice, Heart challenge
- "The Hall of Broken Promises" - Shows every lie you told, Body survival test

**Labyrinth Changes Based on Choices**:
- Spare enemies → Labyrinth becomes less hostile
- Kill enemies → Labyrinth becomes darker, more dangerous
- Help NPCs inside → Unlock "safe rooms"

### Fallacy Integration
- Each labyrinth section is named after a fallacy
- Solving room requires understanding that fallacy
- Late game: Fight "The Embodiment of Circular Reasoning" boss

## 15. Enlightenment vs. Ignorance Paths

### Concept
Two endgame paths that embody the bright-to-dark arc. Inspired by Dark Souls' endings and Nier Automata's existential choice.

### Mechanics

**Path Split** (triggered at ~70% through game):

**Path of Enlightenment**:
- Seek all truth, no matter how painful
- Gain Knowledge, lose Innocence
- Unlock cosmic horror revelations
- Extremely difficult combat (clarity of purpose, but heavy burden)
- Ending: Transcend human condition, become something else

**Path of Ignorance** (presented as "Preserve Your Humanity"):
- Reject forbidden knowledge
- Maintain Innocence, cap Knowledge at 50
- Stay grounded in human experience
- Moderate difficulty (ignorance is armor but limits power)
- Ending: Remain human but unchanged, world's darkness persists

**Third Option** (hidden, requires perfect balance):
**Path of the Absurd Hero**:
- Accept that truth is heavy AND life is worth living
- Balance Knowledge and Authenticity
- Hardest path (no advantages, only your will)
- Ending: Embrace the absurd, find meaning despite meaninglessness
- Unlocks *Sisyphean Resolve* (passive: never give up)

### Fallacy Integration
- **Fallacy**: Willful ignorance, nihilism, absurdism (Camus)
- Choice reflects game's central question: Is it better to know painful truths or maintain comforting illusions?

## Equipment Recommendations

### Fallacy-Themed Equipment (Additions to Existing Compendium)

**The Burden of Proof**:
- Heavy armor set
- Wearer must justify every action verbally (slows turns)
- +10 Mind Defense, -2 Body
- When attacked, can force attacker to "prove" their claim
- Fallacy: Burden of proof, shifting the burden

**The Paradox Ring**:
- Accessory
- Allows simultaneous contradictory states (can attack and defend same turn)
- But causes dissonance after 3 uses
- Fallacy: Logical paradox

**The Unfalsifiable Hypothesis**:
- Shield
- Cannot be proven wrong, therefore cannot be defeated
- Blocks one attack per combat completely
- But makes you intellectually dishonest (-5 Authenticity per use)
- Fallacy: Unfalsifiability

**The Ego's Armor**:
- Light armor
- Deflects Ad Hominem attacks
- +5 Heart Defense
- But blinds you to valid criticism (can't learn from failures)
- Fallacy: Ego defense mechanisms

**The Broken Social Contract**:
- Weapon (club)
- High damage to "authorities" and "institutions"
- Low damage to "individuals"
- Represents rebellion against unjust systems
- Fallacy: Appeal to consequences, anarchism

## Progression Recommendations

### Tutorial Phase (Childhood - Current)
- Light-hearted introduction to mechanics
- Fishing village is idyllic
- Combat is rare, non-lethal
- NPCs are helpful, honest
- Teach basics without darkness

### Act 1 (Entering Labyrinth)
- Tone shift: This is harder than expected
- First required kill (self-defense)
- Innocence starts dropping
- Introduce Authenticity and Dissonance
- First fallacy use that feels "necessary"

### Act 2 (Descent)
- Labyrinth reveals its nature
- NPCs inside are desperate, turn on each other
- Moral dilemmas with no good options
- Authenticity harder to maintain
- Combat becomes grim necessity
- First major revelation (Burden of Knowledge increases)

### Act 3 (Darkness)
- Visual shift to dark palette
- Music becomes oppressive
- Most NPCs are corrupted or dead
- Choice: Give in to darkness or fight it
- Unlock either Virtue Ultimate or Fallacy Ultimate
- Endings diverge based on accumulated choices

## Final Recommendations Summary

### Top 5 Systems to Implement First
1. **Authenticity Metric** - Core to the philosophical theme
2. **Emotional Resonance Combat** - Makes combat meaningful
3. **Socratic Dialogue System** - Makes NPCs engaging
4. **Corruption of Innocence** - Drives the narrative arc
5. **Virtue vs. Fallacy Trees** - Gives progression moral weight

### Philosophy Integration Checklist
Every mechanic should answer:
- ✅ Does it relate to Heart, Body, or Mind?
- ✅ Is it based on a fallacy or virtue?
- ✅ Does it support the bright-to-dark arc?
- ✅ Can it be tied to a real philosopher or concept?
- ✅ Does it force meaningful choice?

### Tone Progression
- **Early**: Optimistic, adventurous, curious (childhood wonder)
- **Mid**: Questioning, uncertain, disillusioned (growing up)
- **Late**: Dark, heavy, existential (confronting harsh reality)
- **End**: Choice-dependent (transcendence, nihilism, or absurd heroism)

## Comparative TTRPG Analysis

### What Axiomancer Does Differently
- **vs. D&D**: Philosophy instead of fantasy, fallacies instead of spells
- **vs. Mörk Borg**: Progression toward darkness instead of starting dark
- **vs. Disco Elysium**: Full combat system, not just skill checks
- **vs. Darkest Dungeon**: Emphasis on intellectual/moral decay, not just stress
- **vs. Persona**: Adults using philosophy, not teens with personas

### Unique Selling Points
1. Fallacy-based combat never done before
2. Bright-to-dark arc is emotionally impactful
3. Philosophical stances as character classes
4. Authenticity as a core stat
5. Every mechanic is a philosophical concept

This TTRPG has the potential to be the "Dark Souls of philosophical RPGs" - challenging, rewarding, and thematically cohesive.
